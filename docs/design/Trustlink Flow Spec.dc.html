Trustlink Flow specification

The sealed quotation loop, end to end

Screen-to-screen journeys for both the posting business and the responding business, with the actions, data handoffs, state transitions, and notification triggers that connect them. No screens are designed in this pass. Eight open decisions at the end need your call before layout begins.

01 — Constraints as design rules

What every screen must obey

Restated as the rules I will design against, so a mismatch is caught here rather than in layout.

Sealed
Contents are invisible to everyone until closing
The respondent sees only their own record. The buyer sees no contents, no identities. At closing, everything releases at once. No screen may preview, rank, or summarise a sealed quotation.
Tamper-evident
Every quotation carries its integrity result
Submission writes a hash to an append-only ledger. At release, each quotation displays whether the recorded hash still matches. A mismatch is flagged and stays visible — never hidden, never silently dropped.
Append-only
Withdrawal adds a record, it does not erase one
A respondent may withdraw and resubmit before closing. After release the buyer sees the withdrawn entry marked WITHDRAWN alongside the replacement. History is part of the credibility record.
No money
The award is the last thing the platform observes
No escrow, invoicing, or payment state. The award screen ends with an explicit handover: the two parties settle directly. Nothing downstream of the award is tracked or displayed.
Observed data only
Seven facts, and nothing invented
Verification status and date, trust tier, requirements posted, requirements awarded, quotations submitted, quotation counts, timestamps. No stars, scores, response-time averages, or completion counts anywhere.
Not social
Trust is documentary, not reputational
No likes, follows, comments, reactions, or activity feed. The home feed is a filtered index of open requirements — the ordering logic is match and closing time, never popularity.

06 — State machines

Every status and what moves it

Three objects carry state. Only two transitions are triggered by a clock rather than a person; both are marked.

Quotation
SUBMITTED → RELEASED → SHORTLISTED → AWARDED | NOT SELECTED · WITHDRAWN
SUBMITTED — respondent seals and submits. Sole state in which withdrawal is possible.
RELEASED — clock trigger. Closing timestamp reached. All quotations on the requirement move together; hash re-checked and stamped VALID or FLAGGED.
SHORTLISTED — buyer action on B6. Optional stage; see decision 05.
AWARDED / NOT SELECTED — buyer award on B7. One awarded; all remaining move to NOT SELECTED in the same transaction, so no respondent is left waiting on a decision already made.
WITHDRAWN — respondent action before closing. New ledger entry; original retained and shown to the buyer after release.
Requirement
DRAFT → OPEN → CLOSED → AWARDED | CLOSED · NO AWARD
DRAFT → OPEN — buyer publishes on B3. Fires match notifications; posted count increments.
OPEN → CLOSED — clock trigger. The same event that releases quotations. Not a buyer action.
CLOSED → AWARDED — buyer award. Terminal; awarded count increments for both parties.
CLOSED · NO AWARD — buyer declines all, or no quotations arrived. Published in "Recently closed", already visible in the feed.
CANCELLED — proposed, pending decision 04. Would apply only while OPEN, be ledger-recorded, and notify respondents who submitted.
Business
UNVERIFIED → PENDING → VERIFIED · TIER 1 → TIER 2 → TIER 3
PENDING → VERIFIED — DTI and BIR records checked. Stamps a verification date and a re-check date twelve months out. Unlocks posting and quoting.
TIER 2 / TIER 3 — additional documents plus awarded-requirement count, per the criteria already stated in the feed sidebar. Fires on award, since award is what moves the count.
EXPIRED — proposed, pending decision 02. The feed already promises a re-check date, so the state it implies must exist and must degrade access somehow.
REJECTED — records did not check out. Needs a re-submission path in onboarding.

08 — Needs your call

Eight decisions before layout

Each one changes a screen. My recommendation is stated; overrule any of them and I will follow yours.

01The live feed contradicts the sealAffects built screens
The home feed currently shows "12 quotations · latest 4 min ago". The count is a system fact and arguably fair. The recency is not: it tells respondents how hot a requirement is running and invites last-minute timing games against a sealed process. Recommendation — keep the count, drop "latest N ago" everywhere. This is an edit to the built feed and the requirement card.
02What happens when verification expires
The sidebar promises a re-check date, so the expiry state must exist. Recommendation — at expiry the business keeps its profile and history but cannot post or quote until re-verified, with a 30-day warning alert. Anything softer makes the verification badge meaningless.
03Can a buyer extend the closing time?
Extending after quotations are sealed lets a buyer wait out a market they cannot see, which weakens the guarantee even though they learn nothing. Recommendation — no extension once any quotation is sealed; extension allowed only while the count is zero. This makes the closing field on B2 a high-weight, confirm-once input.
04Can a buyer cancel an open requirement?
Real procurement gets cancelled — budgets are pulled. Recommendation — yes, while OPEN, recorded to the ledger and shown on the buyer's public profile as a cancelled requirement. Respondents are notified and their sealed quotations are voided unopened. Visibility on the profile is what keeps it from being abused.
05Is shortlisting required before award?
A mandatory stage adds ceremony to a two-quotation requirement. Recommendation — optional. Award directly from released, shortlist when comparing many. B6 then needs both paths without making the direct one feel like a shortcut.
06Browsing while verification is pending
Recommendation — full browse, no posting or quoting. Seeing live requirements in your own category is the strongest argument for completing verification. The alternative, a locked waiting screen, wastes the only moment of high intent.
07Messaging before closing is a side channel
If a respondent can message the buyer freely while sealed, price can be disclosed outside the ledger and the guarantee is theatre. Recommendation — before closing, messages on a requirement are clarification questions only, and the buyer's answers are published to all respondents. Free one-to-one messaging opens at release. This is how public tenders handle it, and it is a real change to the existing chat widget.
08Does the respondent see the other quotations after release?
Full transparency would make Trustlink a price-discovery tool and give MSMEs a real read on their market — but it also exposes every business's pricing to its competitors. Recommendation — after award, respondents see the count and the awarded figure only, never other respondents' identities or figures. Enough to calibrate, not enough to collude.
On approval
Once you have settled these, the build order that de-risks the most is: requirement detail (three states — it defines the card treatment everything else inherits), then quotation submission and its sealed receipt (the mechanism, and the hardest thing to make feel safe), then my quotations, post a requirement, business profile, onboarding, and alerts last, since it is a view onto events the other screens define.
